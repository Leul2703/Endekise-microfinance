Abstract 

The Edekise Microfinance Expense and Loan Tracker System addresses the critical "dual-system 

failure" identified at the Yemsrach Microfinance Hawassa Branch, where the institution suffered 

from the simultaneous burden of obsolete technology and manual process saturation. This project 

presents a specialized digital solution engineered to modernize financial workflows, eliminate 

calculation inaccuracies, and enhance institutional transparency. 

Initial field research revealed that legacy systems were plagued by flawed interest calculation 

logicscheduling repayments based on the last payment date rather than the loan origination 

datewhich led to significant accounting discrepancies. Furthermore, the reliance on physical paper 

archives made data retrieval labor-intensive and error-prone. To mitigate these risks, the Edekise 

system was developed using an Iterative Waterfall Model, ensuring a balance between structural 

discipline and functional flexibility. 

Integrating the future of the institution, the Edekise system is designed for scalability and 

seamless interoperability. A core component of this project is the integration of an automated 

email Gateway for real-time payment notifications , generet report decerease  manual work and  

allowing the Hawassa branch to maintain operations during regional internet outages while 

ensuring data is integrated with the Addis Ababa head office once connectivity is restored. 

Ultimately, this system provides a scalable framework for Yemsrach Microfinance to achieve 

comprehensive digital transformation and maintain strict regulatory compliance with National 

Bank of Ethiopia (NBE) standards

CHAPTER ONE: INTRODUCTION 

The microfinance sector plays a pivotal role in fostering economic development and financial inclusion, 
particularly in developing economies like Ethiopia. By providing financial services to low-income 
individuals and small enterprises, microfinance institutions (MFIs) empower communities, stimulate local 
economies, and alleviate poverty. However, the efficacy of these institutions is heavily dependent on the 
efficiency and inclusivity of their operational systems. 

Yemsrach Microfinance, a established institution operating since 2010 E.C. with its main branch in Addis 
Ababa and six branches including the Hawassa branch in Menahariya, is a key player in this sector. 
Despite its significant role, the institution faces considerable operational challenges that hinder its 
potential for growth and impact. Our team's interview with the branch manager revealed critical 
inefficiencies in their current loan and expense management systems. 

The core of the problem lies in a dual-system failure: technological inadequacy and manual process 
overload. The existing digital system contains fundamental flaws; for instance, it calculates payment 
schedules based on the date of the previous payment rather than the loan's origination date. This leads to 
inaccurate accounting and confusion for both clients and staff. Furthermore, the system is rigid, 
preventing clients from making advance or bulk payments (e.g., paying for two months at once), which 
restricts financial flexibility for customers. 

Compounding this issue is a heavy reliance on manual record-keeping. Client files are stored physically, 
making retrieval of historical data, such as a client's file from the previous year, a tedious and error-prone 
task that requires sifting through hundreds of documents. This lack of digitized, easily accessible data 
cripples reporting, auditing, and customer service efficiency. Additionally, the absence of an automated 
alert system to notify clients of upcoming payment deadlines results in delayed payments and potential 
defaults. 

Beyond operational inefficiencies, there is a significant gap in financial inclusion. Yemsrach's current 
services, which include three saving and five loan products, are not designed to adequately accommodate 
and encourage participation from marginalized groups, particularly persons with disabilities and women. 
This represents a missed opportunity to empower some of the most vulnerable segments of the population 
and integrate them into the marketplace, a crucial step for broader economic development in Ethiopia. 

To address these multifaceted challenges, our project proposes the development of a comprehensive 
Microfinance Expense and Loan Tracker System. This web application aims to automate and streamline 
the core financial operations of Yemsrach Microfinance. It will introduce accurate, date-originated 
payment scheduling, enable flexible payment options, digitize client records for instant retrieval, and 
implement automated SMS reminders for clients. Furthermore, the system will be designed with an 
inclusive framework to better serve and encourage the participation of women and persons with 
disabilities, thereby aligning the institution's operations with the broader goals of economic empowerment 
and inclusion.

Statement of the Problem 

 Microfinance institutions serve as a critical bridge to financial inclusion for low-income entrepreneurs 
and individuals lacking access to traditional banking services (Helms, 2006). The operational efficiency 
of these institutions is paramount to their sustainability and ability to scale their impact. However, many 
MFIs, particularly in developing nations, rely on legacy systems or extensive manual processes that are 
prone to error, inefficient, and incapable of supporting modern financial services. Yemsrach Microfinance, 
operating since 2010 E.C. with six branches, exemplifies this challenge. Its current system creates 
significant operational friction, from inflexible payment processing to cumbersome manual record-
keeping. 

Despite the well-documented benefits of Management Information Systems (MIS) in reducing operational 
risks and enhancing transparency, a significant gap remains in the technological inclusivity of small to 
medium-sized Ethiopian MFIs. Existing literature emphasizes global digitalization trends, yet fails to 
address the specific socio-economic and technical constraints of local institutions like Yemsrach 
Microfinance, which are often forced to choose between prohibitively expensive international software or 
manual, error-prone processes. Current market solutions lack the contextual flexibility required to handle 
unique local payment practices and the specific reporting mandates required by national regulatory bodies. 
Consequently, there is a critical need for a tailored, cost-effective web application that bridges this gap by 
providing a localized, context-aware framework designed specifically for the underrepresented 
microfinance landscape in Ethiopia. 

1.2 Literature Review 

Previous research consistently highlights the transformative impact of digitalization in the microfinance 
sector. Studies by Ledgerwood (1999) and Armendáriz & Labie (2011) emphasize that Management 
Information Systems (MIS) are crucial for tracking loan portfolios, managing client savings, and reducing 
operational risks. Furthermore, recent literature by Duncombe & Boateng (2009) discusses how mobile 
and web-based platforms can significantly improve loan repayment rates and customer satisfaction by 
providing transparency and timely reminders. 

However, a discernible gap exists in the practical application of these systems within the specific context 
of small to medium-sized Ethiopian MFIs. Many available solutions are either prohibitively expensive, 
overly generic for Western markets, or lack the flexibility to handle unique local payment practices and 
reporting requirements. This project aims to fill this gap by developing a tailored, context-aware web 
application that addresses the specific, on-the-ground challenges faced by institutions like Yemsrach 
Microfinance, which remain underrepresented in existing technological research.

1.6. Limitations of the Study 

The limitations of this study are characterized by specific geographical, institutional, and technological 
boundaries that define the project's current scope and operational environment. Geographically, the 
system is exclusively designed for and tested within the specific context of Yemsrach Microfinance’s 
Hawassa branch, a focus necessitated by the need to manage project depth and resource requirements 
effectively while basing the analysis on the branch's unique operational practices1. Consequently, while 
the findings are directly applicable to the Hawassa branch, the system’s suitability for other Ethiopian 
MFIs or additional Yemsrach branches may be limited without further customization2. Technically, the 
application’s performance—particularly regarding database hosting and automated notification services—
is inherently dependent on the reliability of regional internet connectivity and mobile networks3. Because 
these infrastructure factors are external and beyond the control of the development team, occasional 
network outages could temporarily hinder access to the web interface or delay critical automated 
reminders, potentially affecting overall operational efficiency4. To address these constraints in the future, 
the system's architecture can be studied for broader scalability, and the implementation of robust offline 
functionality or redundant notification methods could be explored to mitigate regional connectivity 
limitations5.

CHAPTER TWO : ANALYSIS OF THE EXISTING SYSTEM 

The analysis of the existing system is a critical phase that lays the intellectual foundation for the development of 
the proposed Microfinance Expense and Loan Tracker System. This phase does not aim to fully document every

established institution operating since 2010 E.C. with six branches including Hawassa Menahariyaunderstanding 
the current technological and manual systems is especially important due to the institution’s reliance on both 
digital and traditional procedures and its need to comply with the National Bank of Ethiopia (NBE). 

2.1 Core Purpose of the System Analysis Phase 

The primary objective of this phase is to gain a clear understanding of how Yemsrach Microfinance 
currently performs its saving, loan, and expense operations within the mandated regulatory framework. 
This involves:

understand how daily tasks are executed.

Introduction 

The System Requirements for the Edekise Microfinance system provide the comprehensive blueprint for its 
development and validation, ensuring the final product accurately meets stakeholder needs. These requirements are 
divided into two interdependent sets: Functional Requirements   and Non-Functional Requirements. which define 
the essential quality attributes and operational constraints detailing how well the system must perform. Together, 
these requirements translate the conceptual use case diagram,sequence diagram , activity diagram and Class 
Diagram and user stories into a measurable, verifiable specification that guides design, implementation, and testing 
across all system modules. 
 

3.1 Functional Requirements 

Functional requirements define the core actions and processes the Edekise Microfinance system must perform. 
 
 Account Management 
The system must govern the creation, structuring, and modification of all financial accounts. 

1. Loan Account Creation: The system must provide a user interface accessible only to LoanStaff for 

initiating the createLoanAccount process, requiring mandatory fields such as principalAmount, 

interestRate, and termMonths. 

2. Saving Account Creation: The system must allow SavingStaff to create a new SavingAccount only 

after the Client  established and must automatically apply the current default interestRate and 

depositLimit. 

3. Statement Generation: The system must generate a formal Statement record upon the Client's request, 

reflecting the current state and transaction history of the associated LoanAccount. 

4. Account Closure: The system must allow authorized Staff to transition an Account status from 'Active' 

to 'Closed' archiving all related transaction data while retaining necessary regulatory records. 

 Transaction Processing 
The system must accurately and securely handle all financial movements. 

1. Deposit Validation: The deposit(amount) method for a SavingAccount must validate that the 

transaction amount does not exceed the account's specified depositLimit . 

2. Withdrawal Validation: The withdraw method must first verify that the transaction amount does not 

cause the currentBalance to fall below zero (or any defined minimum balance threshold). 

 Page24



Edekise Microfinance|2026       
 

 

 

3. Balance Update Integrity: All transaction methods (deposit, withdraw, and internal interest 

calculations) must utilize database transactions to ensure atomic updates to the -private currentBalance 

attribute, guaranteeing accuracy. 

 Authentication and Authorization  
The system must ensure proper identity verification and role-based access control (RBAC). 

1. Role Enforcement: The system must strictly enforce that methods designated for specialized roles  are 

inaccessible to users with lower roles  

2. Session Management: After a successful login(), the system must maintain a secure, time-limited 

session linked to the user's userID and role for all subsequent requests until logout() is explicitly called or 

the session times out. 

 Request and Approval Workflow 
The system must manage formal processes for actions that require managerial oversight. 

1. Request Submission: Any high-value action, such as Branch Manager initiating a 

request100KLoanAccount(), must automatically create a new record in the Request entity with the status 

set to 'Submitted'. 

2. Approval Routing: The system must automatically route submitted Request records to the authorized 

approver. 

3. Status Tracking: The Request entity must track the complete history of status changes, including the 

date and the specific user who updated the status using the setStatus(newStatus) method. 

 Process View Implementation (Loan) 

The system must maintain the consistency and accuracy of its data model. 

1. Foreign Key Enforcement: The system must enforce the association that an Account record must 

reference a valid, existing ownerID corresponding to a Client record. 

2. Method Mediation: All modifications to core -private financial attributes  must be exclusively 

processed through the defined +public methods, preventing direct database manipulation.

During the requirement analysis phase, we identified the functional and non-functional 
requirements of the Edekise Microfinance System and produced the analysis model, which 
clearly depicts the problem domain. Now, in this phase, we transition to the solution domain 
in software development. Therefore, we focus on transforming the analysis model into the 
design model, incorporating non-functional requirements and constraints. 

The purpose of designing is to provide a clear direction on how the system will be built and to 
gather sufficient information to drive the actual implementation. It is based on a deep 
understanding of the software model. The objectives of our system design are to model the 
system with high quality. Implementing a high-quality system depends on the nature of our 
design. If the system is designed effectively, it will be easy to make changes to it after 
deployment. Additionally, our project design focuses on decomposing the system into 
manageable parts. This helps us tackle problems in a modular approach. It also describes 
database-related concepts such as relational schema and normalization. 

This chapter outlines the architectural design (three-tier), deployment strategy, database 
design (ER diagram), relational mapping, and normalization to ensure the system handles 
loan origination, payment scheduling from origination dates, advance/multi-month payments, 
digitized client records, automated reminders, and inclusive tracking for marginalized groups.

4.4 Logical View 

The logical view of the Edekise Microfinance System is organized using a layered 
architecture to ensure separation of concerns, maintainability, scalability, and clear 
dependency management. This design aligns with standard enterprise application patterns 
while adapting to the specific needs of a web-based microfinance system, including role-
based access. 

The system is decomposed into logical components: 

 User Management Component — Handles authentication, RBAC and session 
management. 

 Account Management Component — Manages creation, modification, and closure of

 Transaction Processing Component — Validates and processes deposits, withdrawals, 
and repayments with atomic integrity. 

 Workflow & Approval Component — Manages requests and routing to approvers

 Reporting & Reminder Component — Generates statements, system/branch 
performance reports (Admin access), and automated SMS/email reminders. 

 Inclusivity & Compliance Component — Tracks client demographics and enforces 
NBE rules. 

The system is divided into the following logical layers: 

1. Presentation Layer (Application Layer) This layer contains all the boundary classes that 
represent the user interfacesthe user sees. It is responsible for handling user input, displaying 
data, and providing feedback. 

 Includes web pages, forms, dashboards, and components for role-based views. 
 Technologies: HTML, CSS, JavaScript with a framework such as React.js or Vue.js

 Depends on the Business Services Layer for processing user requests.

2. Business Services Layer (Controller / Process Layer) This layer contains all the controller

business rules. 

 Manages core workflows such as account creation, transaction processing, payment 
scheduling from origination date, advance/bulk payments, request submission and 
approvalemail reminders, and loan classification/provisioning. 

 Enforces Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) — ensuring only Admin can view system

 Supports interaction with a Relational DBMS (MySQL or PostgreSQL).

 No direct dependency on presentation or business services — accessed only through

 Supports common functions such as list management, date utilities, validation helpers,

 Promotes code reuse and consistency throughout the application.

4.11 Normalization 

Normalization is a database design technique that aims to minimize data redundancy and 
dependency by organizing data into well-structured tables. The primary goal is to ensure data 
integrity and eliminate anomalies during database operations such as insertion, deletion, and 
updates. The process involves breaking down large, complex tables into smaller, more 
manageable ones, while establishing relationships between them. 

There are several normal forms that define the degree to which a database has been 
normalized. Each normal form represents a level of organization and elimination of 
redundancies. The commonly discussed normal forms include 1NF (First Normal Form), 2NF 
(Second Normal Form), and 3NF (Third Normal Form). 

First Normal Form (1NF) 

The First Normal Form (1NF) is the foundational step in database normalization, ensuring 
that the database is free of duplicate records and that all columns contain indivisible values. In 
the context of the Edekise Microfinance System, achieving 1NF ensures that the data is 
organized into structured tables with unique rows and no repeating groups. 

For this project, the following constraints are applied to ensure compliance with 1NF: 

 Primary Key: Each table includes a unique identifier (primary key) that ensures no 
duplicate rows exist. 

 Atomic Values: All columns in each table store atomic values, meaning each field 
contains a single piece of information. 

 Same Data Type: Each column maintains consistent data types across all rows, 
ensuring data integrity and proper functionality.

values and unique rows. 

 Full Functional Dependency: All non-primary key attributes must be fully functionally 
dependent on the primary key. This means there should be no partial dependency, where 
a non-primary attribute depends only on part of a composite primary key.

5.1 Conclusion 

The development of the Edekise Microfinance Expense and Loan Tracker System represents 

a significant milestone in the digital transformation of Yemsrach Microfinance. This project 

was initiated to bridge the gap between traditional microfinance operations and the demands 

of a modern, data-driven financial landscape. By systematically addressing the "dual-system 

failure" of technological inadequacy and manual process overload identified in our initial 

study, we have created a solution that is both context-aware and technically robust. 

Throughout the development lifecycle, our primary focus remained on solving the core 

operational friction points: the flawed loan calculation logic and the burden of physical 

record-keeping. The implemented system successfully shifts the institution away from rigid, 

error-prone monthly cycles to a flexible framework where loan schedules are accurately tied 

to origination dates. This not only ensures financial transparency for the clients but also 

provides the institution with an accurate reflection of its portfolio at risk, aligned with the 

National Bank of Ethiopia's regulatory standards. 

Moreover, the transition from a paper-heavy environment to a secure, searchable digital 

repository has fundamentally changed the operational speed of the Hawassa branch. Tasks 

that previously consumed hours such as historical file retrieval now occur in seconds, 

allowing staff to reallocate their time toward high-value activities like client relationship 

management and financial counseling. The integration of automated SMS reminders further 

professionalizes the institution, reducing accidental defaults and fostering a proactive 

repayment culture among clients. 

Importantly, this project has pioneered the integration of inclusive design principles in local 

microfinance software. By including specific modules to track and serve women and persons 

with disabilities, we have ensured that Edekise is not just a tool for efficiency, but a vehicle 

for social and economic empowerment.
